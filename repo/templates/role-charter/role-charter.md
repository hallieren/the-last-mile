## 1. What I Am Accountable For (and to Which Level)

```
The outcome I am accountable for on this project:
- Push [the vague ask / project name] to a verifiable production outcome,
  accountable through L3 (real adoption) on the outcome ladder; L4 (business self-sufficiency) is reached through handoff
- Full coverage: discovery, trade-off decisions, eval, prototype to production, adoption and handoff

What I do not promise:
- "Demo success" is not the outcome checkpoint (a demo is a means, not a milestone)
- I do not commit to a timeline that skips security / compliance review
- I do not push to production when the data or the eval does not support it
- I do not commit to [this project's red line, e.g., touching automated payout decisions]
```

**Self-Check**: If you cannot write "accountable to what level" in the first paragraph, you have not yet discussed the definition of the outcome with the sponsor. Talk about that first, then fill in the table.

## 2. Whom I Do Not Replace (Read This to the Sponsor Line by Line)

| I Am Not | Where the Difference Is | What You Still Need Them For |
|--------|----------|------------------------|
| A POC demo team | My checkpoint is the production outcome, not the demo; the demo's target audience is real users, not visiting executives | Product demos for visiting executives are still the business side's job to arrange |
| Outsourced development (a team) | I have an obligation to question every ask (it must answer the workflow claim); I refuse to "shut up and code" | High-volume, clearly-bounded feature development can still be outsourced or handed to the platform team |
| Internal consulting (an advisor) | My advice ships as a working system and gets verified. It does not end with a report | Organizational and strategic-level consulting still needs a dedicated advisor |
| Ops ticket support | I am accountable for the outcome through L3 on the outcome ladder, not just answering a ticket and closing it out; launch is not my finish line | Day-to-day ops tickets and on-call response are still the ops team's job |
| The platform team | I am accountable for this one business unit's field outcome; field findings flow back, but I do not set the platform roadmap | The platform roadmap and cross-department shared feature decisions are still the platform team's job |

## 3. The Two-Sided Expectations Table

| What You Can Expect From Me | What I Need From You |
|----------------|----------------|
| A one-page sync every week, answer first, bad news said right away | A sponsor who can make the call, 15 minutes a week |
| Every AI recommendation in the system comes with a reason, can be questioned, and can be overridden by a human | Real users' time (scoring, shadowing, co-building the eval) |
| I am present during a production incident: response, rollback, post-mortem | De-identified data access and clear data boundaries |
| I teach your people to run and improve the system, instead of leaving behind a black box | A security / compliance reviewer joins the project team from week 2 |
| I say stop when it is time to stop. When the data or the value does not support it, I recommend scaling down or ending it | A consistent way to introduce me (see below); do not call me an "AI expert" |
| Committed effort is written into the charter, with a monthly report on the fulfillment rate | Named commitment and schedule protection from the business side (a person, hours a week, written into their calendar) |
| How the handover works is spelled out before launch, with no gap left | Named post-launch ops ownership and on-call arrangements |
